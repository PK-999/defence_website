Indian defence reference collection

This collection covers Indian defence history, equipment and six gallantry decorations. It combines historical award rosters, recent official announcements, recipient profiles, equipment discovery lists and selected primary-source facts. Its working scope is independent India through 9 September 2026, with earlier service and equipment history retained as explicitly marked context.

The collection is substantial but incomplete. It does not establish a current national total of unique recipients, a complete inventory of the three armed services, or comprehensive biographies and battle histories. Blank fields identify evidence that has not been established. Source observations remain separate from facts suitable for editorial publication.

Coverage

The historical roster contains 4,191 rows from six CSV files in a repository whose recorded commit dates to February 2018. The files contain 21 PVC, 218 MVC, 1,319 VrC, 87 AC, 478 KC and 2,068 SC rows. These counts describe files, not necessarily correctly classified award events. One row in the Vir Chakra file labels Teja Singh’s medal Shaurya Chakra. That conflict remains unresolved. Historical repository

Eighteen Republic Day and Independence Day releases from 2018 through 2026 provide 329 extracted official announcement records for the requested medals. These overlap with other evidence and must not simply be added to the historical total. The records preserve service labels, public service numbers where given, the original rank/name/unit field, announcement dates and explicit repeat-award bars. The series includes civilian and police recipients as well as the three armed services. Republic Day 2018, Independence Day 2026

All 21 Param Vir Chakra recipients have a primary-source narrative brief and a separately attributed set of secondary biographical facts. Across the broader set, 257 linked biography pages were retrieved and 242 yielded selected structured life or service facts. These are not 257 complete biographies. Fifteen recent Army award events have brief citation summaries linked to exact PDF pages. National War Memorial profiles, Army citation document

The equipment collection contains 892 discovery rows spanning Army equipment and infantry weapons, three-service aviation, active Navy craft, historical Navy ships and historical IAF aircraft. The rows mix platforms, variants and individual hulls. They are an acquisition and verification queue rather than an authoritative inventory. Forty-six separately stored primary-source equipment observations include dimensions, dated procurement or commissioning facts, and explicit applicability exclusions.

The conflict and operation collection contains 102 candidate rows, including pre-independence context. Person-to-conflict references and equipment-to-operation links supply a starting relationship graph. Their meaning is deliberately narrow: participation does not establish command, and support deployment does not establish combat use.

Award dates, ranks and identities

An award recipient is a person. An award event is a particular decoration or repeat bar associated with an action and an official decision. One person can have multiple events. A modern profile may show a later rank, while a citation records rank at the action. Both belong in the archive, with dates and evidence.

The data model must retain action date or interval, award approval or announcement date, gazette publication date and investiture date separately. The President’s June 2026 investiture document is a ceremony source; its ceremony date cannot automatically become every recipient’s award year. A citation’s “effective date” also needs its original label preserved. Defence Investiture Ceremony 2026

Official presentation alone does not eliminate transcription errors. Abdul Hamid’s National War Memorial page displays 1967 while its narrative describes the 1965 action. Manoj Kumar Pandey’s header shows 9 July 1999 while the narrative says 3 July. Sanjay Kumar’s header shows 12 December 1999 while the narrative says 4 July. These conflicts are recorded rather than silently resolved. Hamid, Pandey, Kumar

Affiliation also needs more than one field. The 2026 announcement lists Tarun Vasudevan with the Corps of Engineers, while his citation identifies his operational Rashtriya Rifles battalion with Rajputana Rifles. Vipeen Kumar similarly appears with Signals in the announcement and an operational Dogra-associated battalion in the citation. These may describe parent arm and attachment, not contradictory identities. Announcement, Citation pages 6 and 9

Equipment definitions and dated quantities

Equipment dimensions must describe the correct object and configuration. Ship length, beam, draught and displacement require their measurement condition. Aircraft length, wingspan, rotor diameter and folded dimensions cannot share an unlabeled “size” field. Mass needs its condition: empty, normal, maximum takeoff, standard displacement or full load. Performance figures should preserve payload, altitude, fuel and test conditions wherever published.

Dassault gives Rafale family dimensions of 15.30 metres length, 10.90 metres wingspan and 5.30 metres height. These are manufacturer specifications, not proof of a particular Indian weapon fit. Boeing’s present Chinook page explicitly describes CH-47F Block II; its specifications must not be transferred automatically to the Indian configuration. The generic P-8 card likewise does not establish India-specific sensors and communications. Dassault specifications, Boeing Chinook, P-8 product card

The September 2021 Arjun Mk-1A order for 118 tanks is a dated procurement fact. It does not establish deliveries or current holdings. The March 2025 Prachand contracts separately specify 90 helicopters for the Army and 66 for the Air Force. Their combined 156 is an order tranche, not the operational fleet. The same release describes a wet-leased KC-135 training arrangement, which should remain separate from Indian-owned aircraft. Arjun order, Prachand and training contracts

The 2025 Ministry of Defence review supplies commissioning dates for multiple ships and records the IAF MiG-21 retirement on 26 September 2025. Its Rafale naval agreement describes 22 single-seat and four twin-seat aircraft. A catalogue must retain that distinction rather than labeling every aircraft in the order as an identical carrier aircraft. These are dated facts; later changes need new records. Ministry of Defence review

Wars, battles and operational use

A coherent history archive needs a hierarchy of conflict, campaign, operation, battle and action. Repeated operation names require disambiguation by year and theatre. Border confrontations, peacekeeping, counter-insurgency, humanitarian relief, evacuation, exercises and diplomatic support should have explicit types. Membership in a general online conflict list is insufficient to classify an event as an Indian war.

The official historical bibliography points to histories of the 1947–48 Jammu and Kashmir operations, 1965 and 1971 wars, and missions such as Polo, Vijay in Goa, Congo, Korea and Shanti. A bibliography confirms that works exist; it does not substitute for reading them. Those histories are priority evidence for battle chronology, command structure, unit involvement and outcomes. Official history bibliography

The Kargil retrospective supports the start of Operation Safed Sagar on 26 May 1999 and identifies the recaptures of Tololing on 13 June and Tiger Hill on 4 July. These dates refer to particular milestones, not necessarily the complete battle intervals. Historical IAF accounts name several aircraft used in the campaign, but do not establish an identical combat role for every type. Kargil retrospective, IAF campaign account

“Majorly used in which battles” needs a measurable definition. The archive should distinguish documented presence, transport or reconnaissance support, weapons employment and a source-supported decisive contribution. It should not rank equipment by importance merely from mentions in articles. Where a source describes relief deployment, the relationship should say humanitarian assistance rather than combat.

Additional fields needed for a complete reference product

People need name variants and transliterations, service affiliation intervals, rank at action, rank at award, later rank, unit attachments, birth and death evidence, training, career milestones and other decorations. Biographies should distinguish supported facts from disputed anecdotes. Civilian recipients and foreign recipients must remain representable. Portraits and citation images need explicit reuse information.

Award events need the medal’s historical name, repeat-bar ordinal, posthumous status, action location, action interval, gazette number and page, official citation reference, ceremony details and a short original story. A record must also explain which requested fields remain unknown. The historical Ashoka Chakra classes require a documented mapping to later award names before ingestion.

Equipment needs manufacturer, designer, origin, Indian production partner, platform family, variant, hull identity where applicable, role, measurements and units, propulsion, crew, protection, sensors, armament and public capability limits. Procurement, delivery, induction, retirement and loss events should each have dates. Counts require quantity type, unit, service, variant, effective date and uncertainty. Ordered, delivered, active, reserve, leased, retired and museum examples must not be combined.

Historical events need alternative names, location and map precision, command relationships, participating units, chronology, objectives, outcomes, losses with attribution, linked award actions, equipment roles and further reading. Contested casualty or victory claims should retain the claimant and source instead of becoming an unattributed single number.

For the website, immersive navigation can coexist with literal facts. Recipient names, ranks, award names, chronology and source labels should retain their historical meaning. Search should understand common acronyms and spelling variants. Each published page should expose source links, an effective date for changing information, known gaps and a correction route. These are product recommendations, not claims that the corresponding features already exist.

Completion limits

The collection has not reconciled every award against the Gazette of India. Most recipients do not yet have complete biographies or citation stories. The equipment discovery list is not exhaustive, especially for retired Army systems, ammunition, naval weapons, sensors and support equipment. Most dimensions and capability fields remain unfilled pending the correct primary technical source. Battle-level histories and key-figure coverage also remain incomplete.

The official gallantry directory’s collected pages repeat records and cannot support a completeness claim. Its published policy also restricts systematic copying; bulk profile collection was not undertaken. The cached index remains local research evidence. Alternative gazette, service, manufacturer and historical sources are required to close the remaining gaps. Website Policy

The accompanying implementation and verification sequence turns these limits into specific editorial tasks. Integrity checks confirm provenance, extraction and preserved distinctions. They do not certify that every historical statement is correct. Public release requires claim-level review and a reconciled coverage denominator for any collection described as complete.

Param Vir Chakra recipient briefs

These are original summaries of the National War Memorial accounts. Action dates and displayed dates remain separate in the dataset. The linked secondary life facts require independent verification before they form full biographies.

Somnath Sharma

Major · 4 Kumaon · Indian Army · Posthumous

He led his company while it was attacked from several directions near Srinagar. Moving between positions under fire, he directed the defence until a mortar explosion killed him. The memorial credits his leadership with sustaining resistance and delaying the attack. National War Memorial profile.

Jadunath Singh

Naik · 1 Rajput · Indian Army · Posthumous

He commanded a post that faced repeated attacks and suffered severe casualties. Although wounded, he continued resisting and charged the attackers with a Sten gun. He died during the defence. National War Memorial profile.

Rama Raghoba Rane

Second Lieutenant · Bombay Sappers · Indian Army

He led engineers clearing mines and obstructions under shellfire. Wounded during the action, he continued helping tanks negotiate the route. The memorial describes his work as crucial to opening a safe lane for the advance. National War Memorial profile.

Piru Singh

Company Havildar Major · 6 Rajputana Rifles · Indian Army · Posthumous

During an assault on a defended hill, his section suffered heavy losses. He continued attacking machine-gun positions despite wounds and the loss of his companions, dying during the action. National War Memorial profile.

Karam Singh

Lance Naik · 1 Sikh · Indian Army

He kept his section fighting through repeated attacks after bombardment damaged its defences. Despite wounds, he moved between positions, helped comrades and sustained their resistance. His section held the locality. National War Memorial profile.

Gurbachan Singh Salaria

Captain · 3/1 Gorkha Rifles · Indian Army · Posthumous

During a United Nations mission, he confronted Katangese forces near a roadblock. He led a close assault despite being wounded and died from his injuries. The memorial credits the action with protecting the UN headquarters. National War Memorial profile.

Dhan Singh Thapa

Major · 1/8 Gorkha Rifles · Indian Army

He commanded an isolated post facing repeated Chinese attacks. The defenders continued resisting after heavy bombardment and losses. He fought at close quarters before being overpowered. His award was not posthumous. National War Memorial profile.

Joginder Singh

Subedar · 1 Sikh · Indian Army · Posthumous

His platoon resisted successive Chinese assaults. Wounded in the thigh, he refused evacuation and continued directing the defence. When ammunition ran out, he led the remaining men in close combat. National War Memorial profile.

Shaitan Singh

Major · 13 Kumaon · Indian Army · Posthumous

He commanded a company attacked by a much larger Chinese force. Moving between platoon positions under fire, he continued encouraging his soldiers despite severe wounds and died during the battle. National War Memorial profile.

Ardeshir Burzorji Tarapore

Lieutenant Colonel · Poona Horse · Indian Army · Posthumous

He led his armoured regiment through several engagements during the 1965 war. Despite injury, he remained in command as it fought for successive objectives. He died when his tank was hit. The cited actions extend over several days. National War Memorial profile.

Abdul Hamid

Company Quarter Master Havildar · 4 Grenadiers · Indian Army · Posthumous

He commanded a recoilless-gun detachment that engaged Pakistani tanks. Repeatedly changing position under fire, he continued fighting until mortally wounded. The page’s narrative dates this action to 1965, while its displayed header incorrectly shows 1967. National War Memorial profile.

Albert Ekka

Lance Naik · 14 Guards · Indian Army · Posthumous

During an attack on fortified positions, he silenced machine-gun fire obstructing his company’s advance. Despite serious injury, he continued attacking another position. The memorial dates the action to 4 December; the date requires reconciliation with other accounts. National War Memorial profile.

Nirmal Jit Singh Sekhon

Flying Officer · 18 Squadron · Indian Air Force · Posthumous

He took off in a Gnat while Pakistani aircraft were attacking Srinagar airfield. He engaged the attackers despite their numerical advantage. His aircraft was shot down during the fight, and he received the award posthumously. National War Memorial profile.

Hoshiar Singh

Major · 3 Grenadiers · Indian Army

He led his company in capturing a defended position and resisted subsequent counterattacks. Despite wounds, he moved between trenches to direct his soldiers. When a machine-gun crew was hit, he operated the weapon himself. National War Memorial profile.

Arun Khetarpal

Second Lieutenant · Poona Horse · Indian Army · Posthumous

He moved his tanks to reinforce another squadron, fighting through resistance en route. In the subsequent tank battle, he continued engaging the enemy despite injury and orders to withdraw. He died when his Centurion was hit again. National War Memorial profile.

Bana Singh

Naib Subedar · 8 Jammu and Kashmir Light Infantry · Indian Army

He volunteered for an assault on a Pakistani-held position in extreme mountain conditions. His team climbed a difficult ice approach and attacked the defenders at close quarters. The memorial credits his leadership in capturing the post. National War Memorial profile.

Ramaswamy Parameswaran

Major · 8 Mahar · Indian Army · Posthumous

After militants ambushed his returning search column, he organised a counterattack. Shot in the chest during close fighting, he continued issuing orders and inspiring his soldiers until he died. National War Memorial profile.

Manoj Kumar Pandey

Lieutenant · 1/11 Gorkha Rifles · Indian Army · Posthumous

He led attacks on bunkers obstructing his company’s advance in Batalik. Despite multiple wounds, he continued until killed. The narrative dates the action to 3 July, although the page’s header displays 9 July. National War Memorial profile.

Yogendra Singh Yadav

Grenadier · 18 Grenadiers · Indian Army

As part of an assault platoon, he climbed a steep approach under fire and attacked fortified positions. Despite multiple bullet wounds, he continued fighting, helping the rest of the platoon advance. The memorial spells his first name “Yogender.” National War Memorial profile.

Sanjay Kumar

Rifleman · 13 Jammu and Kashmir Rifles · Indian Army

Leading his company’s approach, he attacked an enemy position after coming under fire. Although seriously wounded, he continued fighting with a captured machine gun. The narrative dates the action to 4 July; the header instead displays 12 December. National War Memorial profile.

Vikram Batra

Captain · 13 Jammu and Kashmir Rifles · Indian Army · Posthumous

He led assaults during the Kargil campaign, including actions at Point 5140 and Point 4875. During the latter engagement he continued leading despite serious wounds and died in the fighting. The memorial credits his example with inspiring his troops to capture the objective. National War Memorial profile.
