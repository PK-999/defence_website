# Indian defence reference collection

This collection covers Indian defence history, equipment and six gallantry decorations. It combines historical award rosters, recent official announcements, recipient profiles, equipment discovery lists and selected primary-source facts. Its working scope is independent India through 9 September 2026, with earlier service and equipment history retained as explicitly marked context.

The collection is substantial but incomplete. It does not establish a current national total of unique recipients, a complete inventory of the three armed services, or comprehensive biographies and battle histories. Blank fields identify evidence that has not been established. Source observations remain separate from facts suitable for editorial publication.

## Coverage

The historical roster contains 4,191 rows from six CSV files in a repository whose recorded commit dates to February 2018. The files contain 21 PVC, 218 MVC, 1,319 VrC, 87 AC, 478 KC and 2,068 SC rows. These counts describe files, not necessarily correctly classified award events. One row in the Vir Chakra file labels Teja Singh’s medal Shaurya Chakra. That conflict remains unresolved. [Historical repository](https://github.com/Rishav159/mine_india_gallantry_awards/tree/415893485d81261bcb426b6515dc2b3d65f3fcfe)

Eighteen Republic Day and Independence Day releases from 2018 through 2026 provide 329 extracted official announcement records for the requested medals. These overlap with other evidence and must not simply be added to the historical total. The records preserve service labels, public service numbers where given, the original rank/name/unit field, announcement dates and explicit repeat-award bars. The series includes civilian and police recipients as well as the three armed services. [Republic Day 2018](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=175895), [Independence Day 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2299540&lang=1)

All 21 Param Vir Chakra recipients have a primary-source narrative brief and a separately attributed set of secondary biographical facts. Across the broader set, 257 linked biography pages were retrieved and 242 yielded selected structured life or service facts. These are not 257 complete biographies. Fifteen recent Army award events have brief citation summaries linked to exact PDF pages. [National War Memorial profiles](https://nationalwarmemorial.gov.in/param-yoddhas), [Army citation document](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/aug/doc2026814957801.pdf)

The equipment collection contains 892 discovery rows spanning Army equipment and infantry weapons, three-service aviation, active Navy craft, historical Navy ships and historical IAF aircraft. The rows mix platforms, variants and individual hulls. They are an acquisition and verification queue rather than an authoritative inventory. Forty-six separately stored primary-source equipment observations include dimensions, dated procurement or commissioning facts, and explicit applicability exclusions.

The conflict and operation collection contains 102 candidate rows, including pre-independence context. Person-to-conflict references and equipment-to-operation links supply a starting relationship graph. Their meaning is deliberately narrow: participation does not establish command, and support deployment does not establish combat use.

## Award dates, ranks and identities

An award recipient is a person. An award event is a particular decoration or repeat bar associated with an action and an official decision. One person can have multiple events. A modern profile may show a later rank, while a citation records rank at the action. Both belong in the archive, with dates and evidence.

The data model must retain action date or interval, award approval or announcement date, gazette publication date and investiture date separately. The President’s June 2026 investiture document is a ceremony source; its ceremony date cannot automatically become every recipient’s award year. A citation’s “effective date” also needs its original label preserved. [Defence Investiture Ceremony 2026](https://www.presidentofindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/2023-06/gallantry_awards_8_june_2026.pdf)

Official presentation alone does not eliminate transcription errors. Abdul Hamid’s National War Memorial page displays 1967 while its narrative describes the 1965 action. Manoj Kumar Pandey’s header shows 9 July 1999 while the narrative says 3 July. Sanjay Kumar’s header shows 12 December 1999 while the narrative says 4 July. These conflicts are recorded rather than silently resolved. [Hamid](https://nationalwarmemorial.gov.in/param-yoddhas/details/14), [Pandey](https://nationalwarmemorial.gov.in/param-yoddhas/details/21), [Kumar](https://nationalwarmemorial.gov.in/param-yoddhas/details/23)

Affiliation also needs more than one field. The 2026 announcement lists Tarun Vasudevan with the Corps of Engineers, while his citation identifies his operational Rashtriya Rifles battalion with Rajputana Rifles. Vipeen Kumar similarly appears with Signals in the announcement and an operational Dogra-associated battalion in the citation. These may describe parent arm and attachment, not contradictory identities. [Announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2299540&lang=1), [Citation pages 6 and 9](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/aug/doc2026814957801.pdf)

## Equipment definitions and dated quantities

Equipment dimensions must describe the correct object and configuration. Ship length, beam, draught and displacement require their measurement condition. Aircraft length, wingspan, rotor diameter and folded dimensions cannot share an unlabeled “size” field. Mass needs its condition: empty, normal, maximum takeoff, standard displacement or full load. Performance figures should preserve payload, altitude, fuel and test conditions wherever published.

Dassault gives Rafale family dimensions of 15.30 metres length, 10.90 metres wingspan and 5.30 metres height. These are manufacturer specifications, not proof of a particular Indian weapon fit. Boeing’s present Chinook page explicitly describes CH-47F Block II; its specifications must not be transferred automatically to the Indian configuration. The generic P-8 card likewise does not establish India-specific sensors and communications. [Dassault specifications](https://www.dassault-aviation.com/en/defense/rafale/specifications-and-performance-data/), [Boeing Chinook](https://www.boeing.com/defense/military-rotorcraft/h-47-chinook), [P-8 product card](https://www.boeing.com/content/dam/boeing/v2/products/p-8-poseidon/pdf/P-8-Product-Card-one-sheet.pdf)

The September 2021 Arjun Mk-1A order for 118 tanks is a dated procurement fact. It does not establish deliveries or current holdings. The March 2025 Prachand contracts separately specify 90 helicopters for the Army and 66 for the Air Force. Their combined 156 is an order tranche, not the operational fleet. The same release describes a wet-leased KC-135 training arrangement, which should remain separate from Indian-owned aircraft. [Arjun order](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1757320&lang=1), [Prachand and training contracts](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2116411&lang=1)

The 2025 Ministry of Defence review supplies commissioning dates for multiple ships and records the IAF MiG-21 retirement on 26 September 2025. Its Rafale naval agreement describes 22 single-seat and four twin-seat aircraft. A catalogue must retain that distinction rather than labeling every aircraft in the order as an identical carrier aircraft. These are dated facts; later changes need new records. [Ministry of Defence review](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2210154&lang=1)

## Wars, battles and operational use

A coherent history archive needs a hierarchy of conflict, campaign, operation, battle and action. Repeated operation names require disambiguation by year and theatre. Border confrontations, peacekeeping, counter-insurgency, humanitarian relief, evacuation, exercises and diplomatic support should have explicit types. Membership in a general online conflict list is insufficient to classify an event as an Indian war.

The official historical bibliography points to histories of the 1947–48 Jammu and Kashmir operations, 1965 and 1971 wars, and missions such as Polo, Vijay in Goa, Congo, Korea and Shanti. A bibliography confirms that works exist; it does not substitute for reading them. Those histories are priority evidence for battle chronology, command structure, unit involvement and outcomes. [Official history bibliography](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=177064)

The Kargil retrospective supports the start of Operation Safed Sagar on 26 May 1999 and identifies the recaptures of Tololing on 13 June and Tiger Hill on 4 July. These dates refer to particular milestones, not necessarily the complete battle intervals. Historical IAF accounts name several aircraft used in the campaign, but do not establish an identical combat role for every type. [Kargil retrospective](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2288840&lang=1), [IAF campaign account](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/erelcontent.aspx?relid=50974)

“Majorly used in which battles” needs a measurable definition. The archive should distinguish documented presence, transport or reconnaissance support, weapons employment and a source-supported decisive contribution. It should not rank equipment by importance merely from mentions in articles. Where a source describes relief deployment, the relationship should say humanitarian assistance rather than combat.

## Additional fields needed for a complete reference product

People need name variants and transliterations, service affiliation intervals, rank at action, rank at award, later rank, unit attachments, birth and death evidence, training, career milestones and other decorations. Biographies should distinguish supported facts from disputed anecdotes. Civilian recipients and foreign recipients must remain representable. Portraits and citation images need explicit reuse information.

Award events need the medal’s historical name, repeat-bar ordinal, posthumous status, action location, action interval, gazette number and page, official citation reference, ceremony details and a short original story. A record must also explain which requested fields remain unknown. The historical Ashoka Chakra classes require a documented mapping to later award names before ingestion.

Equipment needs manufacturer, designer, origin, Indian production partner, platform family, variant, hull identity where applicable, role, measurements and units, propulsion, crew, protection, sensors, armament and public capability limits. Procurement, delivery, induction, retirement and loss events should each have dates. Counts require quantity type, unit, service, variant, effective date and uncertainty. Ordered, delivered, active, reserve, leased, retired and museum examples must not be combined.

Historical events need alternative names, location and map precision, command relationships, participating units, chronology, objectives, outcomes, losses with attribution, linked award actions, equipment roles and further reading. Contested casualty or victory claims should retain the claimant and source instead of becoming an unattributed single number.

For the website, immersive navigation can coexist with literal facts. Recipient names, ranks, award names, chronology and source labels should retain their historical meaning. Search should understand common acronyms and spelling variants. Each published page should expose source links, an effective date for changing information, known gaps and a correction route. These are product recommendations, not claims that the corresponding features already exist.

## Completion limits

The collection has not reconciled every award against the Gazette of India. Most recipients do not yet have complete biographies or citation stories. The equipment discovery list is not exhaustive, especially for retired Army systems, ammunition, naval weapons, sensors and support equipment. Most dimensions and capability fields remain unfilled pending the correct primary technical source. Battle-level histories and key-figure coverage also remain incomplete.

The official gallantry directory’s collected pages repeat records and cannot support a completeness claim. Its published policy also restricts systematic copying; bulk profile collection was not undertaken. The cached index remains local research evidence. Alternative gazette, service, manufacturer and historical sources are required to close the remaining gaps. [Website Policy](https://gallantryawards.gov.in/assets/pdf/Website_Policy.pdf)

The accompanying implementation and verification sequence turns these limits into specific editorial tasks. Integrity checks confirm provenance, extraction and preserved distinctions. They do not certify that every historical statement is correct. Public release requires claim-level review and a reconciled coverage denominator for any collection described as complete.
